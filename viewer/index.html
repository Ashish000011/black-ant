The concept in one minute

A 3.97×1020 kg asteroid sits 637,100 km from Earth, 45° between "ahead" and "away from the Sun". Its gravity pulls Earth outward along its orbit; Earth's gravity pulls back on it with the same force, and sunlight-like beams from stations far sunward push on a mirror tied to the asteroid to hold it in place. Nothing touches Earth. Mars is moved first, by a second tow, to 3.64 AU so the two never meet.

The beams never come within 420,000 km of Earth, beyond the Moon. Mirrors in space top up Earth's sunlight as it moves out, so the climate stays as it is today.
